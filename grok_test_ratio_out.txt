* **On Issue 1:** The prosecution evidence consisting of the plain, categorical, spontaneous and frank testimony of the minor victim, corroborated by the medico-legal findings of healed hymenal laceration, sufficiently established carnal knowledge through the moral ascendancy of the father which substituted for the element of violence or intimidation under Article 335 of the Revised Penal Code; the Court held that the revelations of an innocent child whose chastity has been abused merit credence in the absence of strong justifications to the contrary, citing People vs. Rivera, 318 SCRA 317; the defenses of denial and alibi were rejected because they were not convincing to preclude any doubt on the physical impossibility of the presence of the accused at the locus criminis; the trial court's assessment of credibility deserves great respect because the judge had the unique opportunity to observe the deportment of the witnesses; the resentment theory advanced by the accused was deemed flimsy and insufficient to motivate the filing of a complaint for a crime as serious as rape; accordingly, the conviction for rape was affirmed although reduced to simple rape.
* **On Issue 2:** Article 335 of the Revised Penal Code as amended by Section 11 of Republic Act No. 7659 expressly requires the concurrence of the victim's minority and her relationship to the offender as special qualifying circumstances that must be alleged in the complaint or information before the death penalty may be imposed; the constitutional guarantee under Section 1(2), Article III of the 1987 Constitution that the accused be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation renders it essential that every element of the offense, including qualifying circumstances, be properly alleged; because the information dated 29 January 1998 failed to state that the victim was under eighteen years of age and that the offender was her parent, the trial court committed reversible error in sentencing the accused to death; the accused could therefore be convicted only of simple rape punishable by reclusion perpetua, following People vs. Narido, 316 SCRA 131 and People vs. Panique, 316 SCRA 757.
* **On Issue 3:** The term aggravating circumstances in Article 2230 of the Civil Code is understood in its broad or generic sense so that an award of exemplary damages is justified whenever the crime is committed with one or more aggravating circumstances, whether ordinary or qualifying; the Court abandoned its prior case-by-case approach and held that relationship, even when used to qualify rape under Republic Act No. 7659, still entitles the offended party to exemplary damages because the civil liability aspect addresses the victim's personal sufferings separately from the criminal liability aspect that increases the penalty; the ordinary or qualifying nature of an aggravating circumstance is a distinction relevant only to criminal liability and not to civil liability; the retroactive application of Sections 8 and 9, Rule 110 of the 2000 Revised Rules on Criminal Procedure requiring allegation of aggravating circumstances cannot divest the private offended party of vested civil rights already incurred prior to their effectivity; consequently, the accused was ordered to pay P25,000.00 exemplary damages in addition to civil indemnity and moral damages.
